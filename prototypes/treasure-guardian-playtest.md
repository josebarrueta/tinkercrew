# Treasure Guardian family play-test script

## Purpose

Test whether children aged roughly 7, 10, and 14 can contribute meaningfully to one shared mission and whether the loop feels like play rather than a tutorial.

## Preparation

- Open `treasure-guardian.html` directly in a modern browser.
- Use one shared screen and input device.
- Invite three children and one adult facilitator if possible.
- Set aside 30–45 minutes. Do not teach the interface before starting.
- Tell the crew: “You can’t break it. Please say what you are thinking.”
- With permission, take written notes. Do not record identifying information.

## Session

### 1. Crew setup — 3 minutes

Ask each child to enter a first name or nickname and choose Explorer, Inventor, or Hacker.

Observe:

- Do the tracks sound equally valuable?
- Does anyone feel assigned a track only because of age?
- Can the crew explain how the tracks differ?

### 2. Imagine and roles — 5 minutes

Let the crew choose the Sleeping Dragon mission, read the story, and distribute the first roles.

Ask only if needed:

- “Who has an idea for the treasure?”
- “What does your role let you decide?”

Observe whether all children speak or act.

### 3. Build — 5–10 minutes

Use nearby LEGO or drawing materials if available. A quick sketch is enough; the prototype must also work without materials.

Do not suggest a model. Note whether the open build objective is exciting or confusing.

### 4. Connect, code, and test — 10 minutes

Let the crew connect the simulator, set thresholds, and discover the three distance zones. Avoid explaining the correct sequence.

If stuck, remind them that hints are available. Record:

- Which hint level they request
- Whether the hint helps
- Whether automatic checkmarks feel rewarding, restrictive, or unclear
- Whether one child takes over the controls
- Any state or button whose meaning is unclear

### 5. Remix — 5 minutes

Ask the crew to choose and test one personality remix. Then ask:

- “What changed?”
- “Was that your invention or the game’s choice?”
- “What would you rather change?”

### 6. Explain and Free Build — 5 minutes

Have the Reporter collect a short shared explanation and complete the mission. Generate two Free Build prompts and ask which one they would build.

## Closing questions

Ask each child separately where possible:

1. What was the most fun part?
2. What was boring or confusing?
3. Did your role matter?
4. What did another crew member do that helped?
5. Would you play another mission? Why?

Ask the adult:

1. Where did you need to intervene?
2. Which controls or content should be parent-controlled?
3. Did the activity create conflict or cooperation?
4. Was the amount of sound and screen interaction appropriate?

## Success signals

- Every child makes at least one consequential choice.
- The crew completes the mission without a facilitator giving the solution.
- At least one hint helps without ending discovery.
- Automatic checks guide retry rather than create a permanent failure.
- The crew can describe its remix in its own words.
- At least two children want to try another prompt or mission.

## Immediately afterward

Complete `treasure-guardian-findings.md` while details are fresh. Record exact phrases where useful, but no names or identifying information.
